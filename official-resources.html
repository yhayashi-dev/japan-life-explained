Emergency help in Japan

Police

110

For a crime or accident needing police now.

Fire / Ambulance

119

To report a fire or request an emergency ambulance.

Tell the operator what happened and where you are. Give your address or a nearby landmark. Answer the operator’s questions.

For 119, say fire (火事, kaji) or ambulance (救急, kyūkyū).

Emergency at sea: call 118 (Coast Guard).

If Japanese is difficult

Tell the operator which language you speak. Language support varies by service and area. If someone nearby speaks Japanese, ask them to help with the call.

Not an emergency?

For non-urgent police advice, contact your local police station. For a routine clinic visit or local procedures and multilingual life advice, use the relevant guide. These services do not replace emergency services.

For current warnings and municipal evacuation notices, see Disaster and evacuation information. This is separate from calling an emergency service.

Work help: wages, conditions and job search

Choose the service for your question. These are specialist work services; local help covers everyday life and administrative questions.

My wages were not paid

Contact a Labour Standards Inspection Office (労働基準監督署) about unpaid wages or other labour-standards concerns. Ask which office handles your workplace.

Find a Labour Standards Inspection Office by prefecture (Japanese)

I have a question about working conditions

For hours, leave, contract terms or workplace problems, a General Labour Consultation Corner (総合労働相談コーナー) can explain the relevant public service. It can refer labour-standards concerns to an Inspection Office.

Find a General Labour Consultation Corner

I do not understand a deduction

Ask payroll what the deduction is for, its basis and how it was calculated. Check pay after deductions and how it was paid separately; do not assume an unexplained difference is correct.

If you still need advice, use the labour consultation services above or the foreign-language services below.

I am looking for a job

Hello Work offers public job-search advice and job referrals. You can ask about finding work while employed or after leaving a job. Job-search support is separate from employment insurance benefit eligibility.

Hello Work guidance for foreign jobseekers

Need help in another language?

MHLW foreign-language labour consultation directory lists telephone services and local consultation offices. The Telephone Consultation Service has call charges; the Labour Standards Advice Hotline is free. Check the current language, day, hours and contact method before calling or visiting.

For Hello Work, check the linked official guidance for interpretation support at your office. Confirm availability and whether you need an appointment; support is not the same at every office.

This site does not decide whether an employer acted unlawfully or calculate a claim. Ask the relevant public service about your situation.

Find local help

Need help with a local procedure or with understanding Japanese? Start with the municipality where you live.

  1. Check the name of your city, ward, town or village.
  2. Find its official website. Search for your municipality's name plus 市役所 (city office), 区役所 (ward office) or 役場 (town or village office). Check that the result belongs to your municipality.
  3. Look for “foreign residents”, “multilingual consultation” or 外国人相談. The regional directory below can also help you find a consultation center.
  4. On the center's current official page, check languages, days, opening hours, telephone / in-person / online options, and whether you need an appointment. Check who can use the service. These details vary by center; contact it if anything is unclear.
  5. Check whether the office can handle your application or only offer advice. Confirm the correct counter and required documents before you go.

Find a consultation center in your region

CLAIR (the Council of Local Authorities for International Relations) lists consultation centers by region. The directory below is in Japanese. Look for your prefecture or city name; browser translation may help you navigate. Open the center's own site and look for your language. Confirm important details with the center if the translation is unclear. If a directory link is outdated, check your municipality's official site.

Advice, applications and emergencies are different

Consultation: a foreign-resident consultation center can explain procedures or help you find the right service. Do not assume every center supports English, Vietnamese or your preferred language.

Applications and official procedures: address registration and National Health Insurance procedures must be handled through the designated municipal counter or official application route. Receiving advice does not complete an application.

Emergencies: consultation centers are not a substitute for police, fire or ambulance services.

During a disaster, check current official disaster and evacuation information, rather than waiting for an ordinary living-support consultation.

🐱 Cat tip: The exact city office page matters. Garbage rules, address procedures, and resident tax details can differ by municipality.
