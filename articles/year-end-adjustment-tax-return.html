Money & tax

Year-end adjustment or a tax return?

Information checked: September 26, 2026

Your employer has sent tax forms. Does completing them finish your tax paperwork, or do you also need to file a return? Start with the questions below.

Quick answer

For many employees, their employer uses year-end adjustment (年末調整) to settle income tax withheld from salary. A tax return (確定申告) is a separate procedure with the tax office. Some employees must file one; others may file to claim a refund. You can have year-end adjustment and still need a return.

Start with payroll to check what your employer will handle. Check with the tax office if you have other income, an unadjusted job, or a deduction your employer cannot handle.

A simple decision guide

  1. Are you receiving salary? Ask payroll whether you qualify for year-end adjustment and which forms they need. If you are not an employee, check the tax-return rules for your income instead.
  2. Will all relevant salary be included? Mention any previous or second employer. Do not assume one employer handles every job.
  3. Is anything outside the adjustment? Other income, eligible medical expenses or a first-year housing-loan credit can require a separate check.
  4. Still unsure? Take your income records to the tax office. Ask whether filing is required or whether a refund claim may help. This guide is not an eligibility calculator.

What is year-end adjustment?

Tax taken from each salary payment is not necessarily your final annual tax. Your employer compares the amount withheld with the annual income tax, including the special income tax for reconstruction. The adjustment can mean a refund or additional tax.

Eligible employees generally submit the declaration for dependents to their employer. People employed throughout the year, or joining during the year and still employed at year-end, are typical cases. Exceptions and income limits apply. Ask payroll to confirm your eligibility.

For the deduction shown on your monthly statement, see How to read your payslip.

What is a tax return?

A return reports the relevant income and deductions for January to December and settles tax after allowing for amounts already paid. It does not mean paying the same salary tax twice.

The usual filing period is February 16 to March 15 of the following year, with calendar and special-case adjustments. For 2026 income, the usual filing season is in 2027. Check the NTA's notice for that filing season before relying on a specific deadline.

e-Tax lets you submit a return electronically. The NTA's return-preparation service guides you through the figures. Check its current identity-verification and document requirements before starting.

When may year-end adjustment be enough?

Many employees with salary from one employer, correctly adjusted tax and no separate filing requirement do not need a return. Being a foreign national does not, by itself, make a return compulsory. Your income and tax circumstances matter.

An employer completing year-end adjustment does not confirm that every part of your finances has been checked.

When should you check a tax return?

  • High salary, a second job or other income: filing rules depend on amounts and conditions. Check the NTA's wage-earner guidance rather than assuming that all small extra income is tax-free.
  • Salary not subject to Japanese withholding: for example, some overseas-employer arrangements need a separate tax check.
  • Medical expenses: an eligible medical-expense deduction is claimed through a return, not year-end adjustment. Keep records of expenses and reimbursements.
  • A housing loan: if eligible for the housing-loan tax credit, you claim it through a return in the first year. Eligible employees can generally use year-end adjustment from the second year, with the required paperwork.

A return may produce a refund if you paid too much tax, but a refund is not guaranteed. An ordinary optional refund claim can generally be filed from January 1 of the following year for five years. Do not use that rule to delay a required return or a claim with its own deadline.

If you file for a refund, include all income that must be reported. A limited exemption from filing is not the same as tax-free income.

If you changed or left your job

New employer in the same year: your new employer can normally include eligible salary from the previous job in year-end adjustment. Give payroll the previous employer's withholding statement. If payroll cannot verify the earlier salary and withholding, it cannot complete that adjustment; check filing with the tax office.

Left and did not join another employer: most people leaving mid-year do not receive year-end adjustment, although exceptions exist. You may have overpaid income tax and be able to claim a refund. Check your full year's position.

Two jobs at the same time: do not treat this as a simple transfer between employers. Tell payroll and check the separate filing rules.

For the broader leaving-work checklist, see I lost my job: what to do first.

Documents to keep and questions to ask

  • Salary withholding statements (源泉徴収票): keep one from each employer. These record salary, tax and related figures.
  • Employer forms: payroll may request declarations for dependents, the basic deduction, spouse-related deductions and insurance premiums. Forms may combine several declarations. Ask which year each form covers.
  • Supporting records: keep applicable insurance-premium certificates, records of other income, and documents for deductions you plan to claim. Do not claim the same payment twice.

Ask payroll: “Will you do my year-end adjustment? Does it include my previous job? What must I submit, and by when?” Ask the tax office about anything outside that process.

How this differs from resident tax

Year-end adjustment concerns national income tax. Resident tax is a separate local tax, usually based on the previous year's income.

Income-tax return information is used for resident tax, so a separate resident-tax return is generally unnecessary when you file the income-tax return. If you do not file an income-tax return, a municipal declaration may still be needed. Ask your own city or ward; “no income-tax return needed” does not settle that question.

When to ask for individual advice

Ask the tax office if you have foreign income, dependents overseas, possible tax-treaty relief, uncertain tax-residence status, or plans to leave Japan. Tax residence and immigration status are different questions. This article does not decide these cases or calculate your personal tax.

Before you finish

Confirm your employer's role, keep your statements, and check anything outside year-end adjustment. Use the tax office for filing questions and your municipality for resident-tax questions.
