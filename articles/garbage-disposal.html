Garbage in Japan: Find the Rules for Your Address

Information checked: October 4, 2026

Start with your local rules

Garbage rules depend on your municipality. Check the official rules for the city, ward, town, or village where you live. A friend’s collection day or bag may be wrong for your address. This guide is for household waste, not business waste.

1. Check your municipality

Find the municipality name in your home address. Search for that name plus ごみ, or open the municipality’s official website and look for garbage or recycling information. A search result is only a route to the page: check that the page is published by your municipality and applies to your area.

2. Check the collection day for your address

Find the collection calendar or address list for your district, town name or chome. Different parts of one municipality can have different days. Check each waste category’s day, the time to put it out and any holiday changes. Save the current calendar; do not use an old one from a previous home.

3. Check the categories

You may see burnable, non-burnable, recyclable, bottles, cans, PET bottles and oversized garbage. Names and sorting methods differ by municipality. These are examples, not a national sorting list. Use your municipality’s item search or sorting guide for the item you have.

If an item is not listed or you are unsure, ask the municipal waste office before putting it out. Do not assume all plastic or all large items belong in one category.

4. Check bags and the collection point

Check whether your category needs a designated bag, a transparent or semi-transparent bag, a container or another method. Follow the local instructions for preparing the item. Confirm the correct collection point with your landlord or building manager; a nearby collection point may serve another building.

Ask about building-specific arrangements as well as the municipal rules. Put waste out only at the place and time that apply to your home.

5. Oversized garbage: check before taking it outside

Look for 粗大ごみ (oversized garbage). Some municipalities require an advance booking and a fee. Check the item’s eligibility, application method, payment method, collection date and place before moving it outside. Do not simply leave it at the usual collection point.

Size limits and fees are local. Some appliances and other items need a different disposal route; check the official item guide rather than assuming an oversized-garbage booking covers everything.

If you cannot read Japanese

Look for “English”, “Language” or a multilingual living guide on the official website. Some municipalities offer translated calendars or a translation button. If a translated instruction is unclear, ask the municipal waste office or a local foreign-resident consultation desk. Ask what language support is available.

Have your district name and the item’s name or a photo ready. Ask: “Which category is this? Which bag should I use? Where and when should I put it out?” Your landlord or building manager can help you identify the building’s collection point.

Japanese words to search for

  • ごみ — garbage; try your municipality’s name + ごみ.
  • ごみ収集 — garbage collection.
  • ごみ分別 — garbage sorting.
  • 収集日 — collection day.
  • ごみカレンダー — garbage calendar.
  • 粗大ごみ — oversized garbage.

Before you put it out

Check your note: municipality → district calendar → item category → bag or container → place and time. For oversized garbage, also check whether a booking and payment are needed. If any step is unclear, ask before leaving the item outside.
