Disaster and Evacuation Information in Japan

Last checked: October 5, 2026

Check current official information for where you are

Check the municipality and neighborhood of your current location, not only your home address. Use Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) information and your municipality’s current disaster notices. This guide does not show live alerts or decide where you should evacuate.

Need police, fire, an ambulance or help at sea now? Go to Emergency help. Do not delay an urgent call to read this guide.

Five things to check

  1. Current warnings. Open JMA’s multilingual information. Select the relevant area and information: weather warnings, heavy rain or flooding, typhoons, earthquakes or tsunami. Check the issue time.
  2. Your municipality’s evacuation information. Find its official disaster page. Check the affected neighborhoods, issue time and current instructions. A general preparedness page is not a current evacuation notice.
  3. Designated places and shelters. Use the municipality’s hazard maps and evacuation guidance. Check which disaster each place is designated for and current opening information; see the distinction below.
  4. Information you can understand. Look for an official language selector or multilingual notice. Available languages and update times differ. See the official multilingual options below.
  5. Current official instructions. Follow the instructions for your area and the hazard. Do not wait to finish this checklist or install an app before responding to an urgent warning.

Find your local disaster page

Search your city, ward, town or village name plus 防災 (disaster preparedness) or 避難情報 (evacuation information). Open the municipality’s official website and check the relevant district and latest notice. Save the page before a disaster.

For example, Yokohama’s official disaster site links to current disaster information and local maps. This is a local example, not a nationwide service. Find the equivalent for where you are.

Recognize current evacuation terms

Japan’s five alert levels (警戒レベル) are used for risks such as flooding and landslides. Municipal notices include:

  • Level 3 — 高齢者等避難: evacuation for older people and others who need more time, from dangerous areas.
  • Level 4 — 避難指示: an evacuation order; everyone in dangerous areas should evacuate.
  • Level 5 — 緊急安全確保: emergency safety measures when danger is already occurring or imminent. Do not wait for Level 5; it is not always issued.

These are official categories, not a personal safety assessment. Read the current notice for the affected area. JMA’s weather-information names changed on May 29, 2026 to make their corresponding alert levels clearer. Weather information and a municipality’s evacuation notice are different; do not assume a missing municipal notice means there is no danger.

Do not apply this level sequence to earthquakes or tsunami. Tsunami evacuation information does not use these alert levels. Check JMA’s current tsunami information and local instructions without waiting for a numbered evacuation level.

An evacuation place is not always a shelter

Designated emergency evacuation place (指定緊急避難場所) means a place designated to escape immediate danger. Designation depends on the disaster type. A place designated for an earthquake may not be suitable for flooding or tsunami.

Designated shelter (指定避難所) is a facility for people to stay after evacuation. The same facility may serve both purposes, but the terms are not interchangeable.

Before a disaster, check your municipality’s ハザードマップ (hazard map) and nearby designated places. During a disaster, check current local instructions and opening information. Do not assume the closest building, school or mapped shelter is suitable or open.

Multilingual information

JMA’s language selection page provides access to multilingual disaster information. The official Safety tips page explains the multilingual app and download options. Check its current settings and availability before relying on notifications; it does not replace local instructions.

For help finding municipal information before a disaster, use Find local help. Ordinary living-support consultation desks are not the source to wait on for current evacuation instructions.

Useful Japanese search words

防災 — disaster preparedness; 避難情報 — evacuation information; 避難場所 — evacuation place; 避難所 — shelter; ハザードマップ — hazard map; 警戒レベル — alert level. Add your municipality’s name and verify the official source.
