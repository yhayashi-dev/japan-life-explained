Move-in Costs in Japan: Key Money, Deposit, and Fees

Renting an apartment in Japan can require several upfront payments. The first bill may be much higher than one month of rent.

Quick answer

Move-in costs in Japan may include deposit, key money, agency fee, guarantor company fee, first month rent, next month rent, insurance, lock exchange fee, cleaning fee, and other contract-related costs. These are possible items, not charges required for every rental. Amounts and terms vary by property, region and contract. Ask for a written breakdown before signing.

Why the first payment is high

In Japan, the initial payment can include several different fees, and some of them are not returned. This is why the first payment can feel surprisingly high.

🐱 Cat tip: Do not compare only monthly rent. Compare the total initial cost and the renewal cost too.

Common move-in cost items

Deposit

Money held as security for rental obligations. At move-out, ask for an itemized settlement showing any deductions and the remaining balance to be returned. A full refund is not guaranteed.

Key money

A non-refundable payment to the landlord. It is common in some rentals but not all.

Agency fee

A fee paid to the real estate agency for arranging the rental contract.

Guarantor company fee

A fee for a company that guarantees payment if the tenant does not pay rent.

First month / next month rent

You may pay rent for the current month and the next month upfront.

Insurance and other fees

Fire insurance, lock exchange, cleaning, support service, and other fees may appear.

Check the written estimate

Use the actual quote, not a nationwide “months of rent” estimate.

Rent in advance
Which dates does the payment cover? Is a partial month included?
Deposit and key money
Are they charged separately? What are the deposit settlement terms?
Agency fee
Ask for the amount, tax and calculation basis before agreeing.
Other charges
Check which guarantor, insurance, cleaning or other charges apply, when they are due and whether they recur.

Compare the total written estimate, including cancellation and refund terms.

Common misunderstandings

“Deposit and key money are the same.”

No. Deposit may be used for unpaid rent or damage and may be partly returned. Key money is usually not returned.

“The cheapest rent is always the cheapest apartment.”

Not always. Initial costs, renewal fees, and move-out costs can change the total cost.

“The real estate agency owns the apartment.”

Usually no. The agency often introduces the property and handles paperwork, but the landlord owns it.

“All fees are negotiable.”

Some may be negotiable, some may not. Ask politely and get the final breakdown in writing.

What to check before signing

  • Total initial payment
  • Deposit and whether it can be returned
  • Key money
  • Agency fee
  • Guarantor company fee and renewal fee
  • Fire insurance cost
  • Lock exchange and cleaning fees
  • Contract period and renewal fee
  • Move-out cleaning and restoration rules

Questions about move-out costs or a deposit refund?

  1. Compare your rental contract and special terms with your move-in photos, condition checklist and move-out records.
  2. Ask the landlord or management company for an itemized statement: each repair or cleaning charge, its calculation and why it is charged to you.
  3. Check how the charges are deducted from the deposit and whether a balance is refunded or an additional payment is requested.

The MLIT restoration guidelines (Japanese) distinguish ordinary wear and aging from damage caused by the tenant's fault or use beyond normal use. Restoration does not simply mean making the home new again. Contract terms and the facts matter; this guide cannot decide who pays for a particular repair.

If the explanation does not resolve your concern, find a local Consumer Affairs Center (Japanese) for advice about a rental contract or charge. Bring the contract, records and bill. Check opening hours and language support with the center. For help finding local language support, use Local help; it does not replace specialist advice on a disputed charge.

Summary

Move-in costs in Japan are often more than one month of rent. The most important thing is to ask for a written breakdown and understand which costs are refundable, non-refundable, one-time, or recurring. Compare total cost, not only monthly rent.

  • If you are preparing to move in, check electricity, gas and water arrangements.
  • If you need to understand a guarantor company or its fees, read the guarantor company guide.
  • If you need to prepare a payment or contact method, check bank and phone setup.
