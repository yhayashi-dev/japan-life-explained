Work changes

Changing jobs in Japan: residence procedures to check

Information checked: September 26, 2026

You have a new job, or you are planning to leave your current employer. Before starting the new work, check your status of residence, the work it allows, and any notice you must send.

Quick answer

Changing employers does not always mean changing your status of residence (在留資格). But you may need to notify the Immigration Services Agency of Japan (ISA), called Immigration in this guide. Check whether the new work is allowed under your current status.

A notification reports a change. It does not give you permission for a different activity. Highly Skilled Professional and Specified Skilled Worker holders need particular care. The steps also differ for people with a family-based or permanent status.

First check these three things

  1. What is your current status? Read your residence card. Check its expiry date and any designation document attached to your passport. A visa used to enter Japan is not the same as your status of residence.
  2. Does the new work fit that status? Compare the actual duties, not only the job title. Ask the new employer for a written description.
  3. Do you need to report leaving or joining an organization? Check the official notification page for your status and the dates of the changes.

A simple decision guide

  • New employer, work still within your status: check the notification requirement. Do not assume that a change-of-status application is always needed.
  • Different work, a designated employer, or an unclear situation: ask Immigration which permission is needed before starting. Use the special cases below.
  • No next job yet: check the leaving notification now, then check your permitted activities and expiry date. Do not wait for a new job to deal with a notice already due.

Changing employer does not always mean changing status

For example, a person with Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (技術・人文知識・国際業務) may change employers while continuing activities covered by that status. Skilled Labor (技能) also has its own permitted activities. Neither status permits every kind of work.

The employer's name alone does not settle the question. Your actual duties and the conditions of your status matter. If you cannot tell whether the new role fits, ask Immigration before starting it.

Notification to Immigration

This is a procedure for people holding the specified mid- to long-term residence statuses. It is not a rule for every foreign resident.

  • Contracting-organization notification: includes Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, Skilled Labor and Specified Skilled Worker. It covers events such as the end of a contract or a new contract with an organization.
  • Activity-organization notification: includes Intra-company Transferee and Student, among other statuses. It covers events such as leaving or moving to another activity organization.

The official lists also include other statuses and particular Highly Skilled Professional categories. Check the full list for your exact status. An organization's name or address change, or its closure, can also require a notice.

For a covered event, notify ISA within 14 days from the date the event occurs. Leaving and joining may be separate events with separate dates. ISA offers a combined form when reporting both together; do not delay an earlier notice while waiting for the later event.

You can use the electronic notification system, submit the notice at a regional immigration office, or post it to the address in the official instructions. Check the current instructions for your chosen method.

What to prepare

  • Your residence card details, including your status and card number.
  • The relevant leaving or joining date, and the organization's name and address.
  • Details requested by the form, such as your activities at the new organization.

At the counter, show your residence card. For a postal notice, include the required card copy. ISA says supporting evidence such as an employment contract is not required for this notification. That does not mean permission applications have no document requirements.

A renewal application does not replace the notification. ISA's notification Q&A separately says that a notice of the job change is not required when change-of-status permission was granted at that time. Do not treat an application still being processed as permission, or assume this removes a notice already due for an earlier event. Ask Immigration if your timing is unclear.

If the new job is different

If your main activity will fall under another status, check the application for permission to change status of residence (在留資格変更許可申請). Submitting an application is not permission to start the new activity.

If you still hold Student (留学) status and are moving into full-time employment, arrange the appropriate change before starting the work. Student part-time permission is not a substitute. If you already changed from Student to a work status, use the rules for the status you now hold.

Would a certificate help?

A Certificate of Authorized Employment (就労資格証明書) can confirm the paid activities you are already allowed to carry out. It can help clarify whether work at a new employer is within that permission.

The certificate is optional, not a document everyone must obtain when changing jobs. Applying for it does not automatically authorize new work or replace a required change of status. Ask Immigration whether it would help resolve your specific uncertainty.

Check the next renewal too

A job change or notification does not restart your period of stay. Check the expiry date and the current renewal instructions for your status.

New-employer and job-duty documents may be needed at the next renewal. For example, ISA lists additional documents for the first Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services renewal after moving to certain employer categories. Ask the new employer to prepare the documents required for your application; there is no single list for every worker.

If there is a gap between jobs

Keep records of your job search and ask Immigration about your situation before your period of stay expires. Check any required leaving notification separately.

Three months without work does not mean automatic cancellation. For many activity-based statuses, continuously not carrying out the permitted activity for three months or more can be a ground for revocation, unless there is a justifiable reason. Highly Skilled Professional (ii) has a different period under this rule.

ISA gives genuine job-seeking activity as one possible justifiable reason, assessed individually. This is not a guaranteed three-month grace period, and other grounds for revocation exist. Do not use the number to decide how long you may stay or what work you may do.

Special cases to check carefully

Highly Skilled Professional (高度専門職)

Type (i): the organization is designated. Changing that organization requires a change-of-status application even if you will remain a Highly Skilled Professional (i). Check the necessary permission before starting with the new organization.

Type (ii): do not simply apply the type (i) rule. It has a different activity framework, and organization notifications can still be required. Confirm the procedure for your category and proposed activity with Immigration.

Specified Skilled Worker (特定技能)

Changing the accepting organization or the specified field requires a change-of-status permission procedure. Being in the same broad industry does not by itself settle whether a move is permitted; the work category and skills also matter.

Check the new employment arrangements and required notifications with Immigration and the new organization. For type (i), check the support plan as well, involving the registered support organization if one is used. A job-change notice alone is not enough. Use ISA's dedicated Specified Skilled Worker guidance for the detailed requirements.

Intra-company Transferee (企業内転勤)

This status is tied to a transfer to a particular office in Japan. Do not assume that a move to an unrelated employer is covered just because the duties look similar. Check whether a change of status is required.

Permanent and family-based statuses

Permanent Resident, Spouse or Child of Japanese National, Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident, and Long-Term Resident do not have the same restrictions on types of work as activity-based work statuses. These statuses are not on the employer-change notification lists above.

Do not confuse them with Dependent (家族滞在) status, which has different work restrictions. Other residence obligations can still apply; this guide concerns a change of job, not a change in family circumstances.

What your employer handles separately

Employers have separate foreign-worker hiring and leaving reports to Hello Work, subject to the official exemptions. Payroll may also handle employment-insurance and social-insurance procedures. Your employer's report is not the same as your own Immigration notification.

Where to ask

Use the regional immigration office or the Foreign Residents Information Center listed on ISA's procedure pages. Prepare your current status, expiry date, work dates and proposed duties. Ask which notification or application applies and when the new work may start.

Before you start the new job

Check your status, the new duties and any notification deadline. Resolve permission questions with Immigration before starting. Keep the residence check separate from health insurance, pension and tax paperwork.
