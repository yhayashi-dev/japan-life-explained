Work changes

Health insurance after leaving a job

Information checked: September 24, 2026

Quick answer

Ask when your current health coverage ends and when any new coverage begins. Then check four possible routes: a new employer, voluntary continuation, National Health Insurance, or a family member's workplace insurance. Eligibility and cost vary. Contact the relevant insurer or municipality promptly. Do not choose only by comparing the deduction on your old payslip.

First: check the coverage dates

Ask your former employer for the date you lose eligibility for its health insurance. Ask for a document confirming that date.

If you have another job, ask the new employer whether you will be enrolled and when coverage starts. A gap between jobs may require another arrangement.

Do not keep using your former workplace's insurance after your eligibility ends. Having a card or document does not mean the old coverage is still valid. If you need medical care during a change, ask your insurer how to confirm your current eligibility.

For background on the systems and proof of coverage, see Health insurance in Japan.

Compare the four routes

These are possible routes, not four options that everyone can freely choose.

If you are covered by a different system because of age or another condition, ask your municipality which procedure applies. This guide does not cover every special arrangement.

Route 1: a new employer's health insurance

Ask the new employer to confirm enrolment. Do not use the job's start date as a substitute for the insurance start date.

Useful questions include:

  • Will this job include workplace health insurance?
  • What is the start date of my coverage?
  • What proof can I use before the insurance information is updated?
  • Which papers do you need from me?

If you have already joined National Health Insurance, ask your municipality about the procedure when you enter workplace insurance. Check both sides of the change rather than assuming every procedure happens automatically.

Route 2: voluntary continuation

Voluntary continuation, 任意継続, lets eligible former members continue with their previous health insurer for a limited period.

The following conditions are for Japan Health Insurance Association, also called Kyokai Kenpo. If you belonged to a health insurance society, 健康保険組合, ask that society for its own instructions. Do not treat this page as its application guide.

For Kyokai Kenpo, you generally need at least two continuous months of insured membership up to your retirement date.

Eligible Kyokai Kenpo applicants must submit the application to the responsible Kyokai Kenpo branch within 20 days, counted from the day after leaving employment. Postal applications must arrive by the deadline. If the twentieth day is a weekend or public holiday, the deadline moves to the next business day.

Coverage can continue for up to two years, but it can end earlier under specified conditions. Ask about both application and termination procedures.

You pay without the former employer's contribution. However, the premium is not always exactly twice your old deduction. Ask the insurer for an estimate and payment instructions.

Route 3: National Health Insurance

National Health Insurance, 国民健康保険, is arranged through your municipality. Ask whether you need to enrol when your workplace coverage ends.

Act promptly. For example, Shinjuku City requires people who need to join its National Health Insurance to notify the city within 14 days of the event creating eligibility, such as losing workplace coverage. Confirm the relevant date, documents and submission method with your own municipality. Do not assume you have the same deadline as voluntary continuation.

The amount depends on factors including previous income, household circumstances and the municipality's rules. Losing your salary does not necessarily make the first bill small.

Ask for an estimate using your actual situation. If payment is difficult, ask whether any reduction or payment arrangement may apply. Do not assume a reduction is automatic.

Route 4: a family member's workplace insurance

You may qualify as a dependant, 被扶養者, under a family member's workplace health insurance. The insurer must check the conditions.

Ask the family member's employer or insurer what income and household information is needed. Mention benefits or other income when asking. Do not assume that having no salary is enough.

Wait for confirmation of eligibility and the start date. An application alone does not prove that coverage has begun.

Health-insurance dependant status is not the same as pension Category III status. The pension route has separate conditions, including the relationship to the insured person. See the companion guide Pension when your work situation changes.

Compare estimates, not guesses

Collect estimates for the routes you are eligible to use. Ask each office what period its estimate covers and whether any part may change.

A useful note might contain:

  • The insurer or municipality's name.
  • The proposed start date.
  • The quoted premium and the period it covers.
  • Any further documents needed.
  • The application and payment deadlines.

Your old payslip shows what was deducted while you were employed. It does not, on its own, show the cost after leaving. Use How to read your payslip if you need help finding the deduction.

This guide does not identify a cheapest route. Compare confirmed estimates and eligibility before deciding.

What to prepare

Ask the receiving office for its current list. Depending on the procedure, useful information may include:

  • Proof that your old insurance ended.
  • Identity and My Number documents required by that office.
  • The name of your former insurer.
  • Information about income and household members.
  • Details of any new workplace coverage.

Documents accepted by one municipality or insurer may differ from another's. Do not send personal numbers or copies of private notices to this website.

Foreign nationality alone does not decide which route applies. If you are also leaving Japan or changing your residence situation, ask the relevant office about those separate procedures.
