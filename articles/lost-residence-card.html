Residence procedures

Lost or Damaged Residence Card: First Steps

Last checked: October 5, 2026 — replacement procedures and official links.

Start here

Lost or stolen? Note when you discovered the loss, report it to the police and check Immigration’s replacement procedure promptly. A police report does not replace the card.

Still have a damaged card? Keep it and use the separate damaged-card procedure below.

Have a combined My Number / Residence Card (特定在留カード)? Also check the My Number steps below immediately.

This guide covers Residence Cards for mid- to long-term residents. A Special Permanent Resident Certificate has separate procedures; check the official guidance for your document.

Lost, stolen or destroyed: check the deadline

For a Residence Card lost through loss, theft, destruction or similar causes, apply within 14 days of learning that you no longer have it. The starting point is when you learned of the loss, not necessarily when it happened. If you learned of it while outside Japan, the period starts on your first subsequent entry into Japan.

Use the ISA loss replacement procedure (Japanese). If the deadline has passed, contact Immigration promptly; the procedure requires an explanation of the delay.

Police and Immigration have different roles

  1. Record what happened. Note when you noticed the loss and where it may have happened.
  2. Report loss or theft to the police. Keep the report acceptance number for Immigration’s loss/theft statement. Immigration may also request a certificate or other evidence. For destruction in a disaster, check the evidence listed in the procedure.
  3. Check the responsible Immigration office. Use the ISA regional office directory and confirm the office for your home address, application arrangements and documents.
  4. Prepare and apply. Follow the current checklist for your case and apply within the applicable deadline.

Ordinary reporting of a missing card is different from needing urgent police assistance. If police, fire or an ambulance is needed now, see Emergency help.

What to prepare

  • The official replacement application form.
  • Your passport (or certificate of status of residence). If you cannot present it, follow ISA’s instructions for an explanation and identity documents; do not assume it can simply be omitted.
  • One photograph meeting the current ISA specifications. For cards issued from June 14, 2026, the photo exemption is for children under one year old on the date of issue, not everyone under 16.
  • The loss/theft statement and police report number, or the evidence required for destruction. Check whether additional proof is requested.
  • Any other documents applicable to you, including a separately issued permission for activities outside your status, if you have one.

This is a preparation list, not a replacement for the official checklist. Ask Immigration about children, representatives or missing documents. The ISA FAQ (Q66–68) says ordinary loss and qualifying damage replacement are free, and loss replacement cannot be completed by post. Combined-card procedures have separate fee rules.

Damaged or badly soiled card

Significant damage, soiling or damaged IC records use the damage replacement procedure (Japanese). Present the card you still have and check the listed documents.

That procedure has no fixed application period unless Immigration orders you to apply. If you receive that order, apply within 14 days of receiving it. Do not apply the loss deadline to every damaged card. Ask Immigration which category fits your card; voluntary exchange is a different category with different fee rules.

Combined My Number / Residence Card

The 特定在留カード, introduced on June 14, 2026, combines Residence Card and My Number Card functions. Loss needs steps for both functions.

Follow the official My Number loss and temporary-suspension instructions. ISA’s combined-card guidance, Q2-2 (Japanese) also tells holders to report to the police and promptly apply at their regional Immigration office. An ordinary Residence Card is issued first; obtaining a combined card again requires a further application.

For a damaged combined card, ISA lists a combined-card application alongside the damage replacement procedure. Check the current combined-card documents and fee table with Immigration. Do not assume the ordinary-card fee or loss route covers every combined-card case.

If you are outside Japan

The replacement deadline above does not decide whether you can board a flight or re-enter Japan. Read the ISA re-entry FAQ (Japanese) and contact Immigration about your circumstances before travel. It describes a way for a representative to obtain proof of a re-entry permit’s expiry when needed. This guide does not determine your re-entry eligibility or cover passport replacement.

Useful Japanese words

在留カード — Residence Card; 再交付 — replacement / reissue; 紛失 — loss; 盗難 — theft; 汚損 — soiling.

The linked ISA procedures and FAQ are the official sources for this guide. For language support, check the current official contact information. Local help can help with everyday questions and finding support; it does not issue a replacement Residence Card.
