Work changes

Pension when your work situation changes

Information checked: September 24, 2026

Quick answer

When a job ends, check what happens to your pension coverage. A new employer may enrol you in Employees' Pension Insurance. Otherwise, you may need a National Pension procedure, or a procedure through an eligible spouse's employer. If payment is difficult, ask about exemption or postponement. Do not assume unemployment makes registration or payments disappear automatically.

First: check your current and next coverage

Employees' Pension Insurance, 厚生年金, is pension coverage linked to eligible employment. National Pension, 国民年金, is the basic pension system.

Start by asking your former employer when your Employees' Pension Insurance membership ends. If you have a new job, ask the new employer whether you will join and on what date.

Do not assume that every job includes the same pension coverage. If there is a gap between jobs, ask the municipal pension counter or a pension office what procedure is needed.

This guide explains where to start. It does not estimate your future pension or decide every person's eligibility.

Joining through a new employer

Ask the new employer to confirm the pension start date. Tell the employer that you have an existing pension record.

Keep your basic pension number information available. Ask which documents the employer needs. You should not need to work out the pension premium yourself to ask about enrolment.

Your payslip can help you identify a pension deduction. If the deduction or coverage is unclear, ask payroll to explain it.

Registering as a Category I member

Category I, 第1号被保険者, is a National Pension membership category. It commonly applies to residents aged 20–59 who are not covered by Employees' Pension Insurance or as eligible dependent spouses.

If you need to change to Category I after leaving work, submit the registration to your municipality within 14 days, counted from the day after leaving employment. Ask the pension counter if you are unsure whether this applies to you.

Even a short gap between jobs can require a procedure. Do not skip the check because another job starts soon.

Ask what to do if a document is missing. Tell the office your employment end date and which papers you are waiting for. Do not quietly leave the issue until a payment notice arrives.

Checking Category III through a spouse

Category III, 第3号被保険者, is a pension category for an eligible dependent spouse of a covered employee. It is not a category for every family member supported by someone else.

Ask your spouse's employer whether you qualify and which procedure is needed. The employer handles the application route for eligible spouses.

Health-insurance dependant status and pension Category III are different checks. For example, being a family member covered by health insurance does not, by itself, establish Category III pension eligibility.

If the employee supporting a spouse leaves work, the spouse may also need to change pension registration. Ask about both people's records rather than checking only the person who left the job.

What to prepare

Check the office's current requirements. You may need:

  • Your basic pension number document, or the My Number documents required for the procedure.
  • Identity documents.
  • Proof of the date your previous pension coverage ended, if requested.
  • Information about the next employer or the spouse-based route you are checking.

A pension number and a health-insurance membership number are not interchangeable. If you cannot find a document, ask the office how to proceed.

Keep private numbers and document images out of messages to this website. Share them only through an appropriate official procedure.

If paying is difficult

Ask your municipality or pension office about an exemption, 免除, or payment postponement, 納付猶予. These require an application and review. They do not start automatically when you lose a job.

Tell the office that your employment ended. A special unemployment rule may change how your previous income is considered. That does not mean every household qualifies: a spouse's or household head's income may still matter, depending on the application.

Ask which evidence of unemployment is accepted. Separation documents or employment-insurance benefit documents may help support the application.

If you receive partial exemption, a remaining premium may still need to be paid. Read the decision and ask how much to pay. Do not treat a partial exemption as a full exemption.

An approved exemption or postponement is different from simply not paying. Ask the office how the decision affects your pension record. This guide does not calculate that effect or recommend a particular application for you.

Notes for foreign residents

Foreign residents can be subject to Japan's pension enrolment rules. Foreign nationality alone does not make registration unnecessary.

If a social security agreement or another special arrangement may apply, ask a pension office. Bring information about the coverage you already have. This guide does not list all agreement exceptions.

Leaving Japan involves a separate check. Do not assume that all pension contributions can be refunded. The Japan Pension Service has official departure guidance, including information about lump-sum withdrawal payments. Read that guidance before making a decision.

Where to ask

  • Former employer: the end date of workplace pension coverage.
  • New employer: enrolment and the new coverage start date.
  • Spouse's employer: a possible Category III procedure.
  • Municipal pension counter: National Pension registration and exemption applications.
  • Pension office: unclear coverage, special circumstances and pension-record questions.

Ask about available language support before visiting. Write down your work dates and main question so the office can direct you to the right procedure.
