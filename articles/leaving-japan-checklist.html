Leaving Japan: what to check before you go

Information checked: September 26, 2026

Moving your home out of Japan? Start with your departure date, your last day at work and whether you plan to return. These dates can lead to different procedures.

Quick answer

Leaving Japan is not one single procedure. Check municipal registration, taxes, health insurance, pension and residence status separately. Telling your employer does not complete every other step.

This checklist mainly covers moving your main residence overseas, including leaving after quitting a job. A holiday or short trip while keeping your home in Japan is different. If you plan to return, check re-entry permission before departure.

First check these four things

  1. Are you moving your main residence overseas? Tell your municipal office your actual plans. Do not decide your registration only from the length of a trip.
  2. Are you leaving your job? Your employment and insurance may end before your flight. Check the gap between those dates.
  3. Will Japanese tax paperwork or payments remain? A bill or filing obligation may still need attention after departure.
  4. Will you return using your current status of residence? Check your passport, residence card, period of stay and intended return date.

A simple timeline

  1. When planning the move: contact your municipality, payroll and insurer. Ask the tax office and Immigration about any unclear situation.
  2. Around your municipal move-out: follow the local notification window. Check registration, My Number card, municipal insurance and pension procedures.
  3. Before departure: arrange outstanding payments and any tax representatives. Finish any return due before leaving. Confirm re-entry arrangements if needed.
  4. After leaving: keep receiving notices through the arrangements you made. Check any remaining filing duties. Consider a pension withdrawal claim only after checking its conditions and consequences.

These steps do not all happen on the same day. Ask each office which date and deadline apply to you.

Leaving Japan checklist

  • Moving your home overseas? Check the municipal move-out notification and My Number card.
  • Tax remains due, or a return is needed? Check resident tax and national income tax separately.
  • Insurance or employment is ending? Confirm the last day of coverage and any gap before departure.
  • Registered in a pension system? Confirm the coverage procedure and keep your pension record.
  • Returning to Japan? Check the applicable re-entry permission. Otherwise, check the residence-card return procedure.
  • Contracts remain open? Ask each provider about payments, cancellation and use from overseas.

Municipal move-out: start with your local office

If you are moving your address overseas, submit an overseas move-out notification (国外転出届) to the municipality where you are registered. Contact the office before moving for its filing window, accepted methods and documents.

For example, Shinjuku's move-out page accepts notifications from 14 days before moving, or within 14 days afterwards. Use your own municipality's instructions rather than treating this example as every office's service rules.

Ask what identity documents and cards to bring, including your My Number card if you have one. A short visit abroad while maintaining your home in Japan is not the same as moving your address overseas.

Resident tax: leaving does not cancel an existing bill

Resident tax is generally based on the previous year's income and your address on 1 January of the tax year. Moving overseas later in that year does not simply erase the tax due. A notice may arrive after you leave.

Ask your municipality's tax section how to pay any balance and whether you need a resident-tax representative (納税管理人). This person handles the relevant local tax matters for you. Some municipalities offer advance-payment arrangements.

The next tax year's position depends on your circumstances on the next 1 January. Ask the municipality if you retain a home, office or other ties in Japan; do not assume that submitting a move-out form settles every tax question.

For bills and salary deductions, see Resident tax in Japan.

Income tax: check filing and your tax representative

Not everyone leaving Japan must file an income tax return. Ask payroll what it will settle and obtain your withholding statement (源泉徴収票). Year-end adjustment may apply when an employee becomes a non-resident, but it does not settle every kind of income or deduction.

If you must file and leave without notifying the tax office of a tax representative, a return and payment may be due by departure. Contact the tax office before leaving rather than waiting for the usual filing season.

If you need someone to handle national tax matters after leaving, appoint an income-tax representative and submit the appointment notice to the responsible national tax office (税務署). The NTA says to submit it when you appoint the person or by your departure date.

This is separate from the resident-tax representative notification to your municipality. One notification does not automatically cover both taxes. Japanese income received after departure can also require a further filing check.

If you need to understand the two procedures, see Year-end adjustment or a tax return?

Health insurance: leaving work and leaving Japan are different

  • Municipal National Health Insurance: when moving your residence overseas, ask the municipal insurance desk about ending coverage, settling premiums and returning any eligibility confirmation document (資格確認書).
  • Employer health insurance: ask your employer or insurer when coverage ends and which documents to return. Moving abroad while remaining employed can be different from resigning.
  • Quitting before departure: check coverage for the time you still live in Japan after employment coverage ends. Do not leave that gap unchecked because you have booked a flight.

Do not use coverage after your eligibility ends. For the possible routes during a gap, see Health insurance after leaving a job.

Pension: check coverage before deciding about a withdrawal

If you pay National Pension as a Category I member and move your residence overseas, check the end-of-coverage procedure with the municipality or pension office. Employees' Pension coverage depends on your employment circumstances. Your employer handles the loss-of-coverage procedure when you no longer qualify.

For the period between quitting and moving overseas, see Pension when your work situation changes. Keep your basic pension number and contribution records.

A lump-sum withdrawal is not a refund of all contributions

A lump-sum withdrawal payment (脱退一時金) has conditions. These include non-Japanese nationality, at least six qualifying months of contributions or coverage, no address in Japan and no previous entitlement to a Japanese pension. You must also have ended coverage under the relevant Japanese pension systems.

The Japan Pension Service's current instructions require a claim within two years from the date you no longer have an address in Japan. Check the full eligibility rules and your own dates with the pension office.

Receiving the payment removes all Japanese pension coverage periods before the claim. Check your future Japanese pension entitlement and any applicable social security agreement first. An agreement may allow periods in two countries to count towards eligibility. People who already meet the qualifying period for a Japanese old-age pension cannot choose the withdrawal instead.

Tax on an Employees' Pension withdrawal

For a non-resident, tax is withheld from an Employees' Pension lump-sum withdrawal. A tax return using the optional retirement-income taxation procedure may produce a refund; it is not guaranteed. National Pension withdrawals are not subject to this withholding.

Ask the national tax office about the return and an income-tax representative before leaving. Keep the payment decision notice. The pension claim and the tax-refund procedure are separate.

Residence status, re-entry and your residence card

If you plan to return: check permission before leaving. For eligible residence-card holders with a valid passport, special re-entry permission (みなし再入国許可) normally requires return within one year of departure, or by the end of the permitted period of stay if earlier. Tell the immigration officer at departure that you intend to use it.

Special re-entry permission cannot be extended abroad. If it does not cover your planned absence, ask Immigration about an ordinary re-entry permit before departure. Eligibility and exceptions matter; special permanent residents have different rules.

If you leave without re-entry permission, including special re-entry permission: a mid- to long-term resident returns the residence card during departure inspection. Immigration invalidates the card and can return the punched card to you. This is not an instruction for everyone travelling overseas to surrender their card.

A municipal move-out notification is not re-entry permission. Check each separately.

My Number: the number and the card are different

Your My Number normally stays the same, including when you later return to Japan. The My Number card has separate validity rules.

For people without Japanese nationality, overseas move-out requires returning the card to the municipality. The card becomes invalid, but you can receive a punched card back as a record of your number.

The scheme for continuing to use a My Number card while living overseas is for Japanese nationals who complete the required procedure. Do not assume it applies to every foreign resident.

Private contracts and leaving a job

Contact your bank, phone provider, landlord and utility companies. Ask about final payments, overseas contact details and whether you may keep using the service. Conditions depend on the provider and contract.

If you are quitting before departure, use I lost my job: what to do first for employment documents and the separate checks after leaving work. If you are staying in Japan to join another employer instead, use Changing jobs: residence procedures to check.

Before you go

Write down who will handle each remaining payment or notice. Keep copies of your records and the contact details for each office. Complete the steps that apply to your move, rather than assuming one departure procedure finishes everything.
