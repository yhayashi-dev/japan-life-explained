Starting Electricity, Gas and Water in Japan

Last checked: October 5, 2026

Ask before applying

Ask your landlord, real-estate agent or management company what is already arranged. Utilities may already be arranged for your home, or you may need to contact the providers yourself. Check before applying.

Having lights or running water does not, by itself, confirm that your own contract is ready. Check who handles each service and who pays. Arrangements depend on the building, area and contract.

First, check these five things

  1. Is it already arranged? Ask separately about electricity, gas and water. Check what is included in your housing agreement and what you must arrange.
  2. Who is the provider? Ask for the name and official contact page for each service at your address.
  3. When should service start? Give your move-in date and ask when to apply. Keep the provider’s confirmation; do not assume same-day service.
  4. Does anyone need to be present? Check whether a visit is needed, who may attend and the appointment time.
  5. How will you pay? Confirm who receives the bill, which payment methods are accepted and whether payment setup needs a separate step.

Before contacting a provider, have the intended contract holder’s name, full address including building and room number, requested start date and contact details ready. Ask about the accepted payment method. Follow that provider’s current form; requirements are not identical everywhere.

Electricity

Electricity may already be available when you arrive, but you may still need a start-of-service application. If the property manager has arranged it, ask what remains for you to do. Otherwise, contact the electricity provider you will use at that address.

Confirm the provider, start date, contract name, address and payment method. Ask whether a visit or access to the building is needed. Do not assume one electricity company serves every home in Japan. If the service does not start as confirmed, contact the provider or building manager.

Gas

Ask whether the home uses gas, which type it uses and which provider to contact. City gas and LP gas arrangements can differ. Follow the instructions for your building and provider.

Ask about an appointment before move-in. Starting gas service may require a technician to open the supply and carry out safety checks inside the home, with someone present. For example, Tokyo Gas requires attendance for its gas-opening visit. Confirm who may attend and what the provider needs; do not treat this example as every provider’s procedure.

Leave opening work, equipment checks and repairs to the appropriate professionals. This guide does not tell you how to operate gas valves or repair equipment.

A gas smell is not a routine start-service question. Follow the official gas-emergency instructions for your address and contact the responsible gas emergency service promptly. Save that contact before move-in; it may be different from the company’s ordinary application desk. Do not wait for a booked opening visit or try to diagnose a leak yourself. For police, fire or ambulance contacts, see Emergency help; that page does not list every gas emergency contact.

Water

Find the water provider responsible for your address through your municipality or building manager. Some buildings have management-company arrangements. If you must apply yourself, check the provider’s official start-of-service instructions.

Confirm the contract name, full address and start date. Check how to submit the notice, how to pay and whether access or attendance is needed. Online and telephone routes are available in some areas, but methods and deadlines vary. There is no single water company for all of Japan.

Short questions to ask

  • “Is electricity already arranged, or do I need my own contract?”
  • “Which gas company should I contact?”
  • “Do I need to be home when the gas is turned on?”
  • “How do I start water service, and who will bill me?”

If you cannot understand the instructions, ask the provider about language support. For non-emergency help finding a local consultation desk, see Find local help.

Before move-in

Keep a short note for each service: who arranged it → provider → confirmed start date → appointment, if needed → payment. If something is still unclear, contact the property manager or provider before relying on the service.

Official sources — examples, not national procedures

These sources support the checks above. Use your own provider’s current instructions; these organizations do not serve every address.

  • TEPCO: English customer FAQ — starting service and when attendance may be needed.
  • METI: gas safety when moving (Japanese) — confirm the gas type with the gas provider.
  • Tokyo Gas: moving procedures (Japanese) — application details, gas-opening visits and attendance.
  • Tokyo Gas Network: gas emergency guidance (Japanese) and Osaka Gas Network: gas emergency guidance (English) — regional examples, not nationwide emergency contacts.
  • Tokyo Waterworks: English start-service guidance.
  • Yokohama Waterworks: moving-in procedures (Japanese) — online or customer-service-center applications.
  • Kyoto University: 2024 international student handbook, daily life (English/Japanese PDF) — ask which utility charges are included in housing costs. Provider details in an older handbook should be checked against current official pages.
