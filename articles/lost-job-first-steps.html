Work changes

I lost my job: what to do first

Information checked: September 24, 2026

Quick answer

Start with three things: your last day of employment, your insurance end date, and the papers your employer will provide. Contact Hello Work about job support and possible unemployment benefits. Check health insurance and pension procedures separately. Do not wait for a benefit decision before checking these procedures. Tax bills and residence procedures may also need attention.

This guide is a starting checklist for people whose job has ended. It also helps if your contract ended or you chose to leave.

First: write down the dates

Make a short note with:

  • Your last day of employment.
  • The date your employer's health insurance ends.
  • Your next job's start date, if you have one.
  • The date the next employer expects your insurance to start.

Your final working day and your last day of employment may differ. Ask your employer if you are unsure. A new job offer does not, by itself, tell you when insurance begins.

Keep letters from your employer and public offices together. Note who you contacted and what you still need. You do not need to understand every system before asking for help.

Ask your employer for the right papers

These papers serve different purposes. Ask when and how you will receive each one that you need.

PaperWhat it helps you do
Employment separation documents, 離職票Ask Hello Work about unemployment benefits. Check both the employment details and the stated reason for leaving.
A document showing your employment insurance numberHelp Hello Work find your insurance record.
Proof that workplace health insurance has ended, 資格喪失証明書Show the end of your previous coverage when arranging new insurance. Ask the receiving office which proof it accepts.
Salary withholding statement, 給与所得の源泉徴収票Keep a record of salary and income tax. You may need it for your next employer or tax procedures.
Final payslipCheck the final payment and deductions.

For an employee leaving during the year, the employer's deadline to issue the salary withholding statement is within one month after leaving. Ask the employer first if it does not arrive. The National Tax Agency provides guidance for missing statements.

For the final payslip, see How to read your payslip.

For income-tax questions after leaving or changing jobs, see Year-end adjustment or a tax return?.

Contact Hello Work

Hello Work is Japan's public employment service. Ask the office serving your address about job-search support and employment insurance benefits.

The basic allowance, 基本手当, is a benefit for eligible jobseekers. Leaving a job does not automatically start payments. Hello Work checks your insurance history, reason for leaving, and ability and intention to work.

The usual insurance-history requirement is at least 12 qualifying months in the two years before leaving. Certain reasons for leaving may allow a different rule: at least six months in the previous year. Hello Work decides which rule applies to your case.

Tell Hello Work if you cannot start work now because of illness, pregnancy, or another reason. Ask which procedure applies instead of assuming you can claim the usual benefit immediately.

If your separation documents have not arrived, ask your employer and contact Hello Work. If the stated reason for leaving is wrong, explain the problem to Hello Work. Do not assume you must accept an incorrect reason.

Before visiting, check the office's current document list. You may need your separation documents, identity and My Number documents, and details of a bank account in your name. Ask about language support when arranging the visit; availability varies.

Check deadlines without confusing them with payment dates

Unemployment benefits have a waiting period and may have a further restriction period. These do not mean money will reach your account on a promised date.

The seven-day waiting period is part of the benefit procedure. It does not simply start on your last day at work. For resignations on or after April 1, 2025, the usual restriction for leaving without a justifiable reason is one month. Exceptions apply. Ask Hello Work about your own timetable, including any relevant training rules.

Health insurance and pension procedures have separate deadlines. Check those now, even while your benefit application is being considered.

Arrange health insurance and check your pension

For health insurance, start by asking who will cover you after your workplace coverage ends. A new employer, your previous insurer, your municipality, or a family member's insurer may be the right contact.

The companion guide Health insurance after leaving a job explains the options and the deadlines to check. For the basic systems, read Health insurance in Japan.

For pension coverage, ask whether your next job will include Employees' Pension Insurance. If there is a gap, another registration procedure may be needed. The companion guide Pension when your work situation changes explains the routes and payment-difficulty support.

Health insurance and pension are separate checks. Completing one procedure does not prove that the other is complete.

Keep tax and residence notices on your checklist

Leaving work does not mean a resident-tax bill disappears. Ask your former employer or municipality how any remaining tax will be collected. Keep payment notices and check their dates. See Resident tax for the previous-income explanation.

Depending on your status of residence, you may need to notify the Immigration Services Agency. Check the official guidance for your status. Your employer's reporting and your own procedures are not necessarily the same. Also check the expiry date on your residence card.

This guide does not decide whether a new job fits your status of residence. Ask Immigration Services Agency staff when you are unsure. If you are moving, Address registration covers a separate procedure.

If you are changing employers, see Changing jobs in Japan: residence procedures to check.

Where to ask

  • Former employer: employment dates, missing papers, final pay and deductions.
  • Hello Work: job search, benefit eligibility, missing separation papers and disputed leaving reasons.
  • Health insurer or municipality: your next health coverage.
  • Municipal pension counter or pension office: pension registration and payment difficulties.
  • Immigration Services Agency: procedures linked to your status of residence.
